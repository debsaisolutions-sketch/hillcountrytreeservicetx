What's Wrong With My Tree? 7 Warning Signs Every Homeowner Should Know

A tree in your yard is more than just landscaping—it's a living investment that adds shade, beauty, and value to your property. But when something seems off with one of your trees, it can be hard to know whether it's a minor issue or a sign of serious trouble. Understanding the common warning signs can help you catch problems early, before they become costly or dangerous.

1. Discolored or Wilting Leaves

If your tree's leaves are turning yellow, brown, or falling off outside of the normal autumn season, this is often one of the first signs something is wrong. Discoloration can point to several underlying issues, including:

  • Nutrient deficiencies in the soil
  • Root damage or compaction
  • Fungal or bacterial infections
  • Improper watering—too much or too little

Pay close attention to whether the discoloration is spreading evenly across the canopy or appearing in patches, as this can help identify the cause.

2. Cracks, Splits, or Cavities in the Trunk

The trunk is the structural backbone of your tree. Cracks, deep splits, or hollow cavities can indicate decay, storm damage, or disease that's compromising the tree's stability. A tree with significant trunk damage may be at risk of falling, especially during high winds or heavy storms.

3. Fungal Growth at the Base

Mushrooms, conks, or other fungal growths growing at the base of the trunk or on exposed roots are usually a red flag. These fungi often feed on decaying wood, which means the tree may be experiencing internal rot that isn't visible from the outside. This is one of the more serious warning signs and typically requires a professional evaluation.

4. Dead or Falling Branches

Occasional twig drop is normal, but if you're noticing larger branches dying back or falling without an obvious cause like a storm, your tree could be struggling. Deadwood not only signals declining health but also poses a safety hazard to your home, vehicles, and family.

5. Sparse or Thinning Canopy

A healthy tree typically has a full, dense canopy appropriate for its species and age. If you notice the canopy thinning out, with more sky visible through the branches than usual, this can be a sign of root stress, disease, or environmental factors like drought or soil compaction.

6. Leaning or Shifting in the Ground

A tree that has recently started leaning, especially if you notice soil lifting or cracking around the base, may have compromised roots. This is particularly concerning after heavy rain or windstorms and should be inspected promptly, as it can indicate an increased risk of the tree falling.

7. Insect Infestation

Small holes in the bark, sawdust-like material at the base, or visible insects can point to a pest infestation. Certain insects, like borers, can severely damage a tree's internal structure over time. Early detection is key to saving the tree before the damage becomes irreversible.

What Should You Do Next?

If you've noticed one or more of these warning signs, it doesn't necessarily mean your tree is beyond saving. Many tree health issues can be managed effectively with proper care, including:

  • Adjusting watering and fertilization schedules
  • Pruning dead or diseased branches
  • Treating pest infestations
  • Improving soil aeration and drainage

However, some issues—especially those involving structural integrity, trunk decay, or root damage—require a trained eye to properly diagnose. What looks like a minor issue on the surface can sometimes be a sign of a much bigger problem underneath.

When to Call a Professional

Trees are valuable, but they can also become liabilities when their health declines unnoticed.
